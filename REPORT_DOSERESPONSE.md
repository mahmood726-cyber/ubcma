Branch `methods-doseresponse`. Built AdaptShrink-style: from-scratch engines
validated against the R gold standards to ~1e-9, then the AdaptShrink
matched-coverage truth-gate applied to a dose-response estimator, with an honest
negative reported. Mirrors the NMA (`nma/`) and DTA (`truth-recovery-dta/`)
threads. All code under `doseresponse/`.

Greenland-Longnecker / Orsini two-stage (and one-stage) dose-response MA on the
canonical `alcohol_crc` JSS dataset (8 incidence-rate cohorts). Reference numbers
generated by `doseresponse/reference/ref_*.R`.

| quantity | max abs diff vs dosresmeta |
|---|---|
| GL within-study covariance (study 1) | **4.2e-16** |
| linear two-stage REML slope / SE | **2.6e-18 / 3.5e-18** |

| RCS spline two-stage REML coef (multivariate) | **1.3e-09** |
| RCS spline two-stage REML between-study Psi | **1.1e-10** |

The closed-form pieces (GL covariance, GLS first stage, fixed/univariate REML)
match at machine precision; the genuinely multivariate REML (p=2 spline, with a
non-trivial between-study covariance) matches to ~1e-9 -- well past the 1e-6
target. (`pytest doseresponse/test_drma.py` -> 9 passed.)

## Stage 2 -- model-based dose-response NMA (MBNMA)
A network whose treatments are (agent, dose) nodes; each agent's nodes are
constrained to a parametric dose-response curve f_a(dose) with f_a(0)=0 (models:
`nma` saturated, `linear`, `exponential`, `emax`). Built on the verified
`nma_core` contrast machinery, so multi-arm shared-arm correlation is preserved.

| check (synthetic dose network, 45-80 studies) | result |
|---|---|
| saturated MBNMA common-effect vs R `netmeta` -- TE | **max abs diff 9.7e-15** |
| saturated MBNMA common-effect vs R `netmeta` -- SE | **max abs diff 4.9e-16** |
| linear slope recovery (truth 0.12 / 0.30) | mean **0.119 / 0.301** |
| Emax recovery (truth Emax 0.9, ED50 2.0) | median **0.915 / 2.033** |

The saturated dose-response model reduces **exactly** (machine precision) to the
external `netmeta` common-effect fit -- that is the external anchor proving the
network likelihood is correct; the parametric models are constrained
least-squares on the same correct likelihood, checked by parameter recovery.
(`pytest doseresponse/test_mbnma.py` -> 5 passed.)

## Stage 3 -- AdaptShrink matched-coverage bake-off: **HONEST NULL**
Estimand: the pooled linear dose-response **slope** beta. Field-standard baseline
to beat: **two-stage REML DRMA** (the dosresmeta workhorse). Data: incidence-rate
dose-response studies with known truth and **one-sided publication selection**
favouring significant positive trends (`sim_doseresponse.py`); selection biases
the naive pooled slope up by +0.011 (moderate) / +0.016 (strong) on a true slope
of 0.045. 300 reps x {none, moderate, strong}.

**Result: no estimator robustly beats two-stage REML at matched coverage.**
- Paired-bootstrap MCIW0 advantage of AdaptShrink vs REML: none `-0.0003`
  (CI crosses 0, P=0.74), moderate `+0.0010` (CI>0, REML wins), strong `+0.0005`
  (CI touches 0, P=0.03). No robust win in any cell.
- The only `verified_wins` flagged by gate stage G3 (one_stage / two_stage_fixed
  under *no* selection, MCIW0 ratio 0.992) **fail the G4 paired bootstrap**
  (CI[-0.0033, +0.0016]) -- a 0.8% point edge that does not survive resampling.
  The two-stage truth-gate correctly does **not** promote them.

### Why the null (mechanism -- this is the informative part)
1. The selection bias on the slope is **real** but there is **no oracle-free
   bias-corrected member to draw on.** Regression-based small-study correctors
   (PET / PEESE on the slope) are **structurally invalid** for log-RR slopes: the
   per-study slope estimate and its SE share the same Poisson count denominators,
   inducing a spurious positive slope-SE association. They carry large **positive
   bias even under NO selection** (PET +0.13, PEESE +0.066, vs REML ~0). So they
   cannot correct selection bias -- they add it.
2. With only valid (low-bias) members available {two-stage REML, two-stage fixed,
   one-stage}, the AdaptShrink kernel's disagreement penalty leaves them at
   near-equal weight ([0.26, 0.37, 0.37]) and they **coincide** (mean member
   spread SD = 0.0003), so the aggregate **collapses onto the field standard**.
   Robustness is preserved (it never loses by more than a hair, and it neutralizes
   the invalid PET/PEESE if those are fed in), but there is no efficiency to gain.

This is a genuine, mechanistic honest negative -- in the spirit of borrowing
pilot-1/pilot-3. AdaptShrink wins in the NMA and DTA threads because there a
shrinkage target genuinely improves matched-coverage width in a bounded region;
for the dose-response *slope under selection* no such region exists, and the
reason is identifiable (no valid corrector).

## Honest caveats
- **No JAGS** -> no exact Bayesian `MBNMAdose` comparison; netmeta reduction is the
  anchor instead. Documented next step.
- Stage-3 data are **simulated** incidence-rate studies with a Poisson DGP and a
  stylised one-sided selection rule. The null is a statement about *this* family;
  a different estimand (e.g. predicted effect at a target dose) or a real-data
  slice could differ. The DRMA/MBNMA engines themselves are validated on the
  *real* `alcohol_crc` data and against `netmeta`/`dosresmeta`.
- The PET/PEESE structural-invalidity finding is specific to ratio/count effect
  measures where effect and SE share denominators (the classic Egger-on-log-ratios
  pathology); it is not a claim about PET/PEESE for mean-difference outcomes.
- External vendor cross-vendor (Codex/agy) was **not** obtained; the four internal
  confirmations + from-scratch re-derivation stand in for it, as the brief allows.
